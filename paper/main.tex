\title{Name or Value? Cue Conflict Reveals When Chain of Thought Overrides Lexical Priors}

\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\begin{abstract}
Language models are misled by variable names that contradict a program's behavior, but an
accuracy drop cannot show where inside the model the name interferes. Interpretability studies
find that intermediate values are computed during chain of thought, yet in their clean tasks the
variable name and the computed value never disagree. We introduce a cue-conflict test for
multi-step arithmetic in which each problem appears with congruent, neutral, and incongruent
variable names under identical arithmetic, and evaluate two Llama models across four difficulty
levels and three demonstration sets. Without a chain of thought the models read a variable's
value off its name: a congruent name raises accuracy by up to \NUM{facilitationmax} points and an
incongruent one yields the name's value as the answer up to \NUM{lureexcessmax} points above
chance. With a chain of thought the name's value has no measurable effect on the answer at any
level. Linear probes trained on neutral problems show that the lure is decodable only at the
name's own tokens and is erased at the step that writes the value; activation patching localises
the no-chain lure to the early-layer representation of the equation that defines the name.
Chain of thought thus overrides the lexical prior by replacing it, not by suppressing it. What a
chain does not remove is a word-class effect on equation selection, and every effect we measure
is smaller than the effect of which three demonstrations are shown.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Consider a short arithmetic problem written with named variables:
\begin{center}
\texttt{pen=1 + cup, \quad cup=2 + 3; \quad pen=?}
\end{center}
The answer is 6. Now rename the second variable to the word \texttt{two}:
\begin{center}
\texttt{pen=1 + \textcolor{inc}{two}, \quad \textcolor{inc}{two}=2 + 3; \quad pen=?}
\end{center}
The arithmetic has not changed, and the answer is still 6. But the name now says something
false. The word \texttt{two} denotes the number 2, while the variable it names holds the value
5. We call 2 the \emph{lure value} of that name and 5 its \emph{true value}. If a model
answers with the lure, or uses it in a later step, we know the name's meaning reached the
computation.

This is the design used to separate reading from naming in the Stroop task
\cite{stroop1935}, where the word \textsc{red} is printed in blue ink, and to separate shape
from texture in vision models \cite{geirhos2019texture}. We bring it to arithmetic.

Why it is needed here. Models read code and mathematics in which names carry meaning, and
names that contradict the code hurt them: removing or corrupting identifiers degrades execution
prediction \cite{le2025names}, misleading comments cut output prediction by about a fifth
\cite{lam2025codecrash}, and misleading renaming impairs comprehension in a study of humans and
models alike \cite{le2026obfuscated}. None of that says \emph{where} the name interferes. An
accuracy drop is equally consistent with a corrupted input, a corrupted computation, and a
correct computation misread at the output.

Interpretability on clean tasks cannot separate them either. \citet{kudo2026faithful} show,
with linear probes and activation patching on exactly this kind of arithmetic, that the value of
each variable becomes linearly decodable only as the chain of thought writes it. But their
variable names are single letters. A decoded 5 can only have come from the computation, because
no name ever says otherwise. Our incongruent names supply the disagreement, so a probe's output
tells us which source the representation holds.

We build matched sets: the same arithmetic in four naming conditions (Table~\ref{tab:conditions})
at five difficulty levels (Table~\ref{tab:levels}), solved with and without a written chain of
thought, on \NUM{n-models} models. Our finding is that the chain of thought removes the name's
influence by \emph{writing over} it. Without a chain, models read a variable's value off its
name: a congruent name adds up to \NUM{facilitationmax} points of accuracy, and an incongruent
one supplies the answer up to \NUM{lureexcessmax} points above chance. With a chain, the name's
value has no measurable effect at any level or model. Probes show why: the lure is decodable
only at the name's own tokens, and it is gone at the step that writes the variable's value.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item a cue-conflict version of a controlled arithmetic benchmark, with matched twins and
generation rules that keep the lure from being correct for any other reason;
\item a map of where the lure and the true value are decodable, by layer and by token, with and
without a chain of thought;
\item causal evidence, from patching activations between matched twins, for where the name's
influence lives when a chain is absent.
\end{itemize}

\section{Setup}

\paragraph{Task.} We reuse the problems, difficulty levels and prompt format of
\citet{kudo2026faithful}. A problem is a list of equations over the digits $0$--$9$ with $+$ and
$-$, ending in a query. Every variable's value is itself a single digit. Three worked examples of
the same level precede each problem. Table~\ref{tab:levels} gives one problem per level. Level~3 is our main
level: two steps, one forward reference, and the level they analyse across models.

\paragraph{Naming conditions.} Each problem is generated once and then rendered in the
conditions of Table~\ref{tab:conditions}. Arithmetic, operators and digits are identical across
a set; only names differ. In the \emph{congruent} and \emph{incongruent} conditions exactly one
variable, the \emph{target}, is named by a number word. Its value is the target's true value in
the congruent condition and something else, the \emph{lure value}, in the incongruent one. The
target is either the queried variable or the intermediate one, and we report the two separately,
because a name on the variable you are asked about is not the same manipulation as a name on one
you must compute on the way.

\paragraph{Generation rules.} Only one variable per problem carries a number word. The lure
differs from every variable's value and from every digit in the problem, so a lure answer cannot
be right for another reason. Names are single tokens in every context of the template under each
model's tokenizer, so twins have the same length and the same token positions (Appendix~A). The
\emph{irrelevant lure} condition puts the number word on a distractor the answer does not depend
on, separating the presence of a number word from a conflict at a variable the model must use.

\paragraph{Regimes.} In the \emph{chain-of-thought} regime the model writes the worked steps
shown in Table~\ref{tab:levels} and then the answer. In the \emph{direct} regime it writes only
\texttt{pen=6}. The comparison between the two is the centre of the paper.

\paragraph{Demonstration sets.} Which three worked examples are shown turns out to matter more
than any name, so every behavioural number below is computed for three fixed demonstration sets
and reported as their mean. We call an effect reliable only when its sign is the same in all
three sets and a cluster bootstrap over matched problems excludes zero.

\paragraph{Probes.} A linear probe reads a chosen variable's value from the residual stream, one
probe per position, layer and seed, with \citeauthor{kudo2026faithful}'s recipe. Probes are
trained on neutral problems only, so they never see a number word as a name; what they report on
an incongruent problem is a property of the representation, not of the probe. Each is paired
with a control probe trained on labels that are a fixed random function of the variable's
\emph{name} \cite{hewitt2019control}. Where the control also scores highly, the probe is reading
name identity rather than a value, and we report it as such.

\paragraph{Patching.} To test whether a name causes a behaviour we replace the activations at
its tokens with the matched twin's and re-read the output \cite{zhang2024patching}. Two controls
accompany every test: swapping one neutral word for another bounds the damage any swap does, and
swapping one lure for a different lure shows whether the answer follows the name.

\section{Results}

\paragraph{Replication.} On the letter condition our per-token probes reproduce
\citeauthor{kudo2026faithful}'s Table~3 for Llama-3.2-3B exactly: the queried variable becomes
decodable at equation 5 of the chain and the intermediate at equation 2, with pre-chain maxima
of \NUM{replication-pre-v1} and \NUM{replication-pre-v2} (theirs: 17.8 and 33.2).

\paragraph{RQ1: behaviour.} Table~\ref{tab:behavior} reports, per model and regime, accuracy
and the pre-registered contrasts pooled over three demonstration sets, with a contrast claimed
only when its sign holds in every set. Without a chain the name's value is used as a shortcut:
a congruent name on the queried variable adds 4.8 points for
Llama-3.1-8B at level 3 and up to \NUM{facilitationmax} points at level 5, and an incongruent
name yields lure answers 1.2 points above chance at level 3 and up
to \NUM{lureexcessmax} points where accuracy is at floor. With a chain the same contrasts are
within \NUM{chain-lure-bound} points of zero in every cell, and congruent and incongruent names
move together. The irrelevant-lure control at level 4 separates presence from binding: a number
word on the distractor costs accuracy but produces no lure answers. What the chain does not
remove is a word-class effect: a number-word name on the queried variable at level 4 costs
Llama-3.2-3B 10.5 points whether or not its value agrees, and the
error analysis shows the model restating the wrong equation next.

\paragraph{RQ2: representation.} Figure~\ref{fig:heat} maps the probe margin over layers and
positions P2--P5 in the incongruent condition, chain of thought beside direct answer. At P2, the
end of the defining equation, the true value is already partly computed (accuracy

the query, a neutral-trained probe reads the lure in \NUM{p3-lure} of instances, but the control

\section{Related Work}

\citet{kudo2026faithful} established when sub-answers are computed during chain of thought on
clean arithmetic; we add cue conflict to ask what that computation is sensitive to. Two recent
studies bring the Stroop design to language models: \citet{wang2026stroop} pits a local
redefinition of a word against its familiar associate and restores the lost margin by patching
from a matched neutral prompt; \citet{hu2026conflict} read a congruency effect off next-token
probabilities in a colour-rule task every model solves. Both measure one next token; neither has
errors, a chain of thought, or probes over reasoning steps. Faithfulness has been measured from
the outside by perturbing prompts, chains or parameters
\cite{turpin2023unfaithful,lanham2023measuring,chen2025reasoning,tutek2025unlearning}; we
measure it inside the model under a cue whose true and lure values are both known. On
arithmetic, \citet{liu2026readout} show that small models copy the trailing chain number and
\citet{prabhakar2024deciphering} that an output prior can override a correct chain; our
late-readout outcome is stated against a positional-copy control. Misleading identifiers and
comments hurt on real code \cite{le2026obfuscated,le2025names,lam2025codecrash}; we trade
realism for localisation. Binding work asks how a value attaches to a name
\cite{feng2024binding,wu2025binding,gurarieh2026mixing}; we ask whether the attachment survives
a name carrying a competing value. \citet{shi2023distracted} add irrelevant context; here the
distractor is the name itself. The design is \citet{stroop1935} and \citet{geirhos2019texture}.

\section{Conclusion}

A misleading variable name is used when the model has nothing better: without a chain of
thought the name's value becomes the answer, with a chain it is replaced at the step that
computes the value and never reaches the output. For faithfulness this is the reassuring cell of
the outcome table, and it comes with two cautions: the chain leaves a word-class effect on which
equation the model attends to next, and the demonstration set moves accuracy more than any name.

\section*{Limitations}

The task is synthetic arithmetic with short chains, so the results need not transfer directly to
real code, which is the motivation and not the finding. Models are at most 8B parameters;
within-family comparisons give the direction of the trend only. Lures are hand-picked English
number words; digit-bearing names such as \texttt{x2}, other languages and optimised lures are
future work. Linear probes can miss nonlinearly encoded information; the patching experiment
partly addresses this, but hidden states carry mixed information and patching cannot rule out
side effects entirely, which is why two control patches are reported. The chain-of-thought
format writes the name beside the computed value at every step, which may itself aid recovery;
the direct regime bounds that effect from the other side.

\section{Tokenizer checks}
Rule~4 is decided per model, not by eye. For each model we tokenize every candidate name in the
five contexts of the template (line start, after a comma, after each operator, before the
separator) and keep only words that are a single token in all five with no merging into a
neighbour. For the Llama~3 tokenizer, all ten number words pass and 95 of 112 neutral candidates
pass; the lists are released with the code. Binary operators carry a space on each side because
\texttt{-} otherwise merges with a following word (\texttt{1-two} tokenizes as \texttt{1},
\texttt{-two}), which would break the alignment between twins. With the released lists, every
instance of a condition tokenizes to the same length with the same position indices, and the
runner excludes and counts any instance that does not; no instance was excluded in any run
reported here.

\section{Full sweeps and selectivity}
For every (variable, position, layer, seed) we report probe accuracy for the true value, lure
rate, lure mass and margin, alongside the accuracy of a control probe trained on labels that are
a deterministic random function of the variable's name \cite{hewitt2019control}. Selectivity is
the difference. The query position is the one cell where a high lure rate coincides with control
accuracy near~1, which is why it is reported as name identity rather than as a represented
value. Per-token sweeps over the whole instance, with the max-over-layers curve, are given for
both regimes and all three conditions, together with $t^{*}$, $t^{*}_{\text{eq}}$ and the
pre-/post-chain accuracies in \citeauthor{kudo2026faithful}'s format.

Chain of Thought Reduces Errors from Misleading Names in Arithmetic
Anonymous ACL submission
Abstract001
Asking language models to write out arith-002
metic calculations reduces answers that follow003
a misleading variable name. Building on Kudo004
et al. ’s arithmetic study, we test thirteen mod-005
els on pairs of problems with the same equa-006
tions. In one version, a variable has an ordi-007
nary name; in the other, its name is a word for008
an incorrect digit. When asked only for the ﬁ-009
nal answer, models more often return the digit010
suggested by the misleading name. Asking011
them to write the calculation before answering,012
known as chain-of-thought prompting, reduces013
this tendency. In 43 of 44 comparisons, the014
added error rate meets our 2-percentage-point015
threshold for a small effect. Writing the calcu-016
lation therefore makes answers more robust to017
misleading wording while the underlying math018
remains unchanged.019

Can asking the model to calculate before an-033
swering reduce this error? In our answer-only con-034
dition, the model is prompted to return just the ﬁ-035
nal digit. In our chain-of-thought (CoT) condition,036
it is prompted to restate and evaluate the equations037
before giving that digit. Here the calculation in-038
cludes cup=2+3=5 and four=1+5=6. The compari-039
son concerns what the model generates; an answer-040
only response can still involve internal computa- 041
tion. 042
Misleading names already reduce accuracy on 043

Kudo et al. (2026) showed that language models can compute answers while generating intermediate steps in arithmetic. Building on this work, we ask whether misleading words can pull written reasoning away from the correct computation. We change variable names in arithmetic problems and Python programs while leaving the computations unchanged. Writing out calculations reduces answers suggested by misleading names, but errors remain possible in both settings. In code, the correct value is recoverable from internal activity with 95.5–99.7% accuracy immediately before the model writes a conflicting value suggested by the name. Changing worked examples to show each source operation alongside its result sharply reduces these mistakes. Adding text of the same length leaves large errors, and extra arithmetic helps only partly. Correct information can therefore be available inside a model without appearing in its written reasoning. Demonstrating the calculation behind each step improves resistance to misleading cues in the tested tasks.
